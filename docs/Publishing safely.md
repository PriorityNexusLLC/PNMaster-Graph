# Publishing safely: how Priority Nexus LLC protects you

Plain-language guide. This is a safety net built on long-standing journalism practice, **not legal advice**; I'm not
a lawyer. The aim: everything you publish is accurate, attributed to the public record, fair, and correctable.
Those four things are what protect publishers.

---

## The idea behind all of it

The strongest protection a publisher has is **truth you can prove**, written as **what the record says**, with
**the other side given a chance to answer**. Many U.S. states also protect **fair and accurate reports of official
public records** (often called the *fair report privilege*), which is why every Priority Nexus finding reads
"*Filings report X as director of Y*" and links the filing, instead of "X is…". You're reporting what the official
record says, not making your own claim.

## What the system now does for you, every time

| Step | What happens | Why it protects you |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Draft (version 1)** | You click **Draft report**. Marked "DRAFT, do not publish". | Nothing goes out unchecked. |
| **2. Independent review (version 2)** | Runs automatically right after. Re-opens **every** source filing and confirms it names both parties; confirms no record changed; scans the wording; checks the safety sections; adds a plain-language glossary. | Catches mistakes and risky words before anyone else sees them. |
| **3. Review notes (just for you)** | Lists each problem with the line, *why* it's risky, and *what to write instead*. | You learn the safe wording as you go. |
| **4. Right of reply** | You contact each organization named; you write the date and their answer in section 6 of your working copy; click **Re-check**. | Fairness, and their answer goes in the report. |
| **5. Verdict** | **READY TO PUBLISH** only when sources, records, wording, structure, reply and integrity all pass. | A clear gate: if it isn't green, it doesn't go out. |
| **6. Fingerprints** | Each version's fingerprint goes into your tamper-evident log. | You can prove exactly what you published, and when. |

**The four verdicts:**
- **BLOCKED: do not publish.** A source didn't check out, a record vanished, or the text contains a legal conclusion
  ("illegal", "fraud", "violated"…). Fix it first.
- **NEEDS ATTENTION.** Loaded or motive words, private details, or a record changed since the draft. Fix, then re-check.
- **VERIFIED: next step is the right of reply.** The report is sound; now contact the organizations.
- **READY TO PUBLISH.** Everything passed, and every organization answered or had 14 days to.

## Words the review stops, and what to write instead

| Don't write | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| corrupt, fraud, illegal, criminal, violated, guilty, bribe, laundering | Only a court or regulator can reach these conclusions | "Filings report…", plus "the standard [name] restricts…" |
| conspiracy, collusion, cabal, cartel, rigged, puppet, scheme | Implies secret wrongdoing you'd have to prove | Describe the documented connection |
| secret, hidden, concealed | These filings are public | "filed with the SEC on [date]" |
| in order to, intended to, deliberately, knowingly | States a motive; filings show roles, not intentions | Leave the motive out |
| clearly, obviously, proves, without a doubt | Overstates certainty | Let the filings speak |
| conflict of interest | That's a finding | "a conflict signal under [named standard]" |
| home addresses, phone numbers, emails, family, health, religion | Private life, not public roles | Remove it |

Quotes from the organizations' own replies are theirs, so the review doesn't flag words inside quotation marks.

## Your habits (the system can't do these for you)

1. **Publish only "READY TO PUBLISH" versions**, the *reviewed* file, never the draft.
2. **Write to every organization before publishing.** Use the suggested message in section 6. Send it to their press
   or media-relations contact (public on their website). Wait 14 days, or until they answer. Keep a copy of what you sent.
3. **Publish their answer in full**, or "did not respond by [date]".
4. **Correct openly and fast.** If someone shows an error: thank them, check it, publish a dated correction at the
   top, and keep the old version available. Never quietly edit.
5. **Stick to public roles.** Directors, officers and owners, in those roles. Nothing about families or private life.
6. **Don't add opinion to the report.** If you want to share your view, do it separately and label it as opinion.
7. **Save every source** with the Internet Archive's Save Page Now (web.archive.org) before publishing.
8. **Keep your records**: the report, its review notes, your reply emails and the audit anchors. They show your care.

## Free and low-cost help (no lawyer budget needed)

- **Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press**: free **legal hotline** for people doing reporting
  (rcfp.org → "Legal Hotline"). Ask them for a referral for pre-publication review.
- **Law school First Amendment / media law clinics**: several U.S. law schools run clinics that help independent
  journalists for free. Ask the Reporters Committee which one serves your state.
- **Anti-SLAPP laws**: many states let a court quickly dismiss lawsuits meant to silence reporting on public issues,
  and sometimes make the filer pay costs. The Reporters Committee can tell you if your state has one.
- **Media liability insurance**: once you publish regularly, a policy for independent journalists is worth pricing;
  some press associations offer member rates.

## The False Claims Act hold (government money)

Your rule: **if a report looks like a possible False Claims Act matter, it is not published until a lawyer has been
consulted.** If no lawyer will take it on contingency or pro bono, the report waits until you can afford one.

**What triggers the hold.** The review watches for a report moving into government money: federal contracts or grants,
Medicare/Medicaid, billing or invoicing an agency, reimbursements, kickbacks, "false claims". Wording that accuses
anyone of defrauding the government is blocked outright. The verdict becomes:

> **ON HOLD: possible False Claims Act matter, consult a lawyer before anything is published**

**Why it matters.** The False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§3729–3733) is the federal law against knowingly billing the
government falsely. New fraud of that kind is reported through a sealed whistleblower case (*qui tam*) that a lawyer
files for you. **Publishing first can bar that case** (the "public disclosure bar", §3730(e)(4)), and accusing anyone
of fraud in public is high risk for you.

**While on hold:**
1. Don't publish it, post about it, or send the draft to anyone, including the organizations named.
2. Keep everything: the report, its sources, your review notes.
3. Look for a False Claims Act whistleblower lawyer. Many work on **contingency**: paid only from what the government
   recovers, so nothing up front; a successful whistleblower can receive 15–30% of the recovery. Whistleblower-support
   organizations, such as the Government Accountability Project or the National Whistleblower Center, and the
   Reporters Committee hotline can point you to lawyers and pro bono help.
4. If no lawyer will take it on those terms, the report simply waits. Nothing is lost; it stays fingerprinted in your log.

**Lifting the hold.** Only after a lawyer has advised you, add this one line to your working copy and press
**Re-check**:

`Legal consult: YYYY-MM-DD, [lawyer or organization], cleared for publication`

The review records the consult; the report then goes through the normal checks.

**Already-public outcomes are different.** When the Department of Justice has *already announced* a settlement or
judgment, that announcement is itself a public record. A future update can add those records, reported exactly as DOJ
states them, including "no determination of liability" when the release says so.

## When something goes wrong

- **Someone complains:** stay calm and polite, don't argue in public, ask what exactly they believe is wrong, check
  it against the filing, and correct if needed. Reply in writing and keep copies.
- **You receive a legal letter:** don't publish anything new about it, don't delete anything, and call the Reporters
  Committee hotline the same day.
